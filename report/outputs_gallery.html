Task 1: universal denoising autoencoder

Twelve representative test examples

report/figures/task1_udae_examples.png

What it shows: Columns: clean target, corrupted input, restored output, absolute error map. One typical image per condition and severity.

How to read it: On the error map, black means no error and bright yellow means a large error (fixed scale 0 to 0.5).

What it means: Noise and blur are repaired with diffuse small errors on edges and fur. Occlusion boxes are filled with plausible colour blends, so the error concentrates in the box.

Four failure cases

report/figures/task1_udae_failures.png

What it shows: The lowest-SSIM test image of each condition.

How to read it: Same columns as above.

What it means: The cat on a black background is 'repaired' even when clean, because black areas look like occlusion masks to a network that is not told the corruption. The flower-field dog shows dense texture that cannot pass the bottleneck. This failure motivates Task 2.

Quality before and after (PSNR)

report/figures/task1_udae_psnr_bars.png

What it shows: For each condition and severity: PSNR of the damaged input (blue) and of the restored output (orange).

How to read it: Orange above blue means the model helped. Higher is better.

What it means: Strong damage improves a lot (noise, high occlusion). Clean and mild blur get worse: the output is capped near 25 dB by the bottleneck.

Quality before and after (SSIM)

report/figures/task1_udae_ssim_bars.png

What it shows: Same as the previous bars, using SSIM (0 to 1).

How to read it: Higher is better.

What it means: Same story as PSNR; for low occlusion the SSIM is slightly lower than the input because the model softens the whole image.

Training curves of the final run

report/figures/task1_udae_curves.png

What it shows: Training loss, validation objective, validation PSNR and validation SSIM per epoch (80 epochs).

How to read it: The first two should fall, the last two should rise.

What it means: Steady convergence, no overfitting (validation keeps improving with training), smoother as the learning rate decays.

Optuna search for Task 1

report/figures/task1_udae_optuna.png

What it shows: Left: the score of every trial (crosses are trials stopped early) and the best so far. Right: which hyperparameters mattered.

How to read it: Lower is better on the left. Longer bars on the right mean more influence.

What it means: 30 trials, 19 completed, 11 pruned. The learning rate mattered most (0.67), then dropout and alpha.

Skip-connection ablation: examples

report/figures/task1_udae_skip_examples.png

What it shows: The same twelve examples for the model with one 16x16 skip connection.

How to read it: Compare with the no-skip examples: look at fur and edges.

What it means: Sharper outputs. The skip lets detail bypass the bottleneck, which is why we investigated it, and why we keep the skip-free model for the 'genuine bottleneck' requirement.

Skip ablation: failure cases

report/figures/task1_udae_skip_failures.png

What it shows: Worst cases of the skip model.

How to read it: As above.

What it means: The black-background problem persists, so it is not only a bottleneck effect.

Skip ablation: PSNR bars

report/figures/task1_udae_skip_psnr_bars.png

What it shows: Before/after PSNR for the skip model.

How to read it: As above.

What it means: Higher orange bars than the no-skip model for every condition.

Skip ablation: SSIM bars

report/figures/task1_udae_skip_ssim_bars.png

What it shows: Before/after SSIM for the skip model.

How to read it: As above.

What it means: Higher than without the skip.

Skip ablation: training curves

report/figures/task1_udae_skip_curves.png

What it shows: Curves of the skip model's final run.

Task 2: classifier, specialists, hard routing

Classifier confusion matrix (test set)

report/figures/task2_test_confusion.png

What it shows: Rows are the true condition, columns the classifier's answer; each row sums to 1.

How to read it: A perfect classifier is a bright diagonal with zeros elsewhere.

What it means: Almost perfect. The small off-diagonal numbers are low occlusion called clean and clean called blur or occlusion.

Classifier confusion matrix (validation set)

report/figures/task2_classifier_val_confusion.png

What it shows: Same matrix on the validation set, for the epoch that was kept.

How to read it: As above.

What it means: Used for choosing the checkpoint; it agrees with the test result, so the model generalises.

Classifier training curves

report/figures/task2_classifier_curves.png

What it shows: Cross-entropy, accuracy (training and validation) and validation macro-F1 per epoch.

How to read it: Loss falls; accuracy and F1 rise towards 1.

What it means: Converges within about 10 epochs; training and validation stay close, so no overfitting. One early dip (epoch 6) while the learning rate was high.

Optuna search for the classifier

report/figures/task2_classifier_optuna.png

What it shows: Trial scores (validation macro-F1) and hyperparameter importances.

How to read it: Higher is better here.

What it means: 12 trials, 3 completed, 9 pruned. Dropout, weight decay and learning rate matter; the network size hardly does (the small preset won).

Task 3: soft mixture-of-experts

Routing heatmap (the key figure of Task 3)

report/figures/task3_moe_routing_heatmap.png

What it shows: Average weight given to each of the four branches (columns) for each true corruption and severity (rows).

How to read it: Brighter means more weight. A bright diagonal means the gate sends each corruption to its own expert.

What it means: Each corruption goes to its own expert and the routing sharpens as severity grows. For mild damage the leftover weight goes to the identity branch, never to a wrong expert.

Weight distribution per corruption

report/figures/task3_moe_weight_distribution.png

What it shows: Box plots of the four branch weights for each true corruption.

How to read it: The box is the middle half of the images; the line inside is the median; dots are outliers.

What it means: Shows the spread across individual images, not just the average: sharing is image-by-image, not a constant split.

Examples where one expert dominates

report/figures/task3_moe_dominant_examples.png

What it shows: Four images where the gate is very sure; row labels give the four weights (id, sp, bl, oc).

How to read it: A weight near 1.00 means that branch alone does the work.

What it means: Strong corruptions get a single expert.

Examples where the weight is shared

report/figures/task3_moe_distributed_examples.png

What it shows: Four images with the most evenly spread weights.

How to read it: Look at the weights in the row labels.

What it means: Mild or ambiguous cases (a low occlusion on a black cat, light noise on grass) share weight, and mixing keeps their detail.

Twelve representative examples

report/figures/task3_moe_examples.png

What it shows: Clean, corrupted, restored, error map with the weights.

How to read it: As in Task 1.

What it means: The soft mixture's typical behaviour across all conditions.

Failure cases with routing weights

report/figures/task3_moe_failures.png

What it shows: The worst image of each condition, with the weights in the row labels.

How to read it: Compare the weights with the true corruption.

What it means: Separates routing errors (clean cat sent 0.87 to the occlusion expert) from expert errors (correct routing, but the expert cannot recover the content).

Quality before and after

report/figures/task3_moe_psnr_bars.png

What it shows: PSNR of input and output per condition and severity.

How to read it: Orange above blue means it helped.

What it means: Best of the three systems on mild corruptions, and clean images stay almost intact.

Training curves

report/figures/task3_moe_curves.png

What it shows: Loss parts, validation objective, gate accuracy and the mean weight of each branch per epoch (epochs 1-2 warm-up, then joint fine-tuning).

How to read it: Mean branch weights should stay away from 0 (no collapse).

What it means: The objective falls through fine-tuning; no branch collapses; the identity weight rises as the gate learns to hedge.

Optuna search for Task 3

report/figures/task3_moe_optuna.png

What it shows: Trial scores and importances.

How to read it: Lower is better.

What it means: 8 trials, all completed, none pruned for collapse; importances are spread evenly.

Task 4: face-to-sketch conditional GAN

Style swap (proof that the style works)

report/figures/task4_cgan_style_swap.png

What it shows: Five test photos, each drawn in Style 1, Style 2 and Style 3.

How to read it: Compare columns for the same row.

What it means: Same identity, pose and hair, but a visibly different drawing style: light outline, heavy shading, soft tone. The learned style embedding controls the output.

Test examples

report/figures/task4_cgan_examples.png

What it shows: Photo, true sketch and generated sketch, three examples per style.

How to read it: Compare the last two columns.

What it means: The generator reproduces structure and style; stroke details differ from the artist's.

Four failure cases

report/figures/task4_cgan_failures.png

What it shows: The four test pairs with the lowest SSIM (all Style 2).

How to read it: Compare true and generated sketches.

What it means: Recognisable and in the right style, but the artist's individual hair strokes sit elsewhere. These failures are partly a limit of pixel-wise metrics.

Training curves

report/figures/task4_cgan_curves.png

What it shows: Discriminator real and fake loss, generator adversarial loss, generator L1, validation SSIM and objective per epoch.

How to read it: Healthy GAN training: the losses oscillate but do not blow up.

What it means: The discriminator slowly gains the upper hand (normal), nothing collapsed, and validation SSIM was still creeping up at epoch 100.

Optuna search for Task 4

report/figures/task4_cgan_optuna.png

What it shows: Trial scores and importances.

How to read it: Lower is better.

What it means: 8 trials, 3 completed, 5 pruned; the balance between the two learning rates and dropout matter most.
